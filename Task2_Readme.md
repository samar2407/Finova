# Task 2: Consensus, Explained Through a Story

## Concept chosen: Consensus

## Format: a short story

### The Puzzle

Every evening in a village, four farmers brought their grain to be
weighed and recorded: Aryan, Ravi, Samar, and Jay. For years, one clerk kept the
official record book. It worked fine, until people noticed the clerk had been quietly
changing numbers to favor his cousin's farm. Nobody could prove it as it was his book,
and he could write whatever he wanted.

So the village tried something different. No single record keeper. Instead, every
evening, the four farmers were given the same riddle: a random number between 1 and
60, and had to guess it. Whoever guessed right first got to write that day's entry in
the book.

But writing it down wasn't enough on its own. The other three farmers had to check it
too, if the grain weight really what was written. And if the right person delivered it.
Only once at least three of the four agreed would the entry actually count.

The first evening, Ravi guessed the number first and wrote "40 sacks from the north
field." Aryan, Samar, and Jay walked over, checked the scale, and nodded, yes, that's
right. Entry confirmed. The next evening, Samar guessed first, and the same process
played out.

Someone asked, "Why not just let whoever's fastest handle every entry? Save time." An
older farmer answered: "Because then we're back to trusting one person again. The
moment only one person's word counts, they can start bending the truth, and by the
time anyone notices, it's too late to undo it. But if it takes the group to agree, no
single farmer can quietly rewrite history."

Within a few weeks, nobody argued about the record book anymore. Not because everyone
had suddenly become more honest, but because no single person could lie without three
others noticing.

---

### What this is actually describing

This is how a blockchain reaches **consensus**. There's no single record-keeper, no
central bank, no one company controlling the ledger. Instead, many independent
participants race to solve a puzzle to earn the right to add the next entry, and that
entry only becomes official once most of the others independently verify it's correct.
No individual participant has to be trusted, it's the agreement of the majority that
makes the record trustworthy.

**Why this matters:** it's the same reason the village stopped trusting one clerk. A
system where one party controls the record is only as honest as that one party. A
system where the majority has to agree means no one can quietly rewrite the truth
without the rest of the group noticing.
